# Getting started

DiskLens needs macOS 13 or later and runs on Apple Silicon and Intel.

## Install

Open the downloaded disk image and drag DiskLens to Applications. If macOS asks you to confirm the first launch, open System Settings, choose Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway.

## First run

A three-step guide opens the first time:

1. **Welcome.** What DiskLens does, in three points.
2. **Full Disk Access.** DiskLens explains why macOS hides some folders and opens the right System Settings pane. You can continue without it. See [Full Disk Access](/docs/full-disk-access).
3. **First scan.** The scan starts by itself and shows files, bytes and the six categories filling in. When it finishes you land on the Overview.

## The three pages

- **Overview** shows your disk, how much is safe to clean, and your largest folders.
- **Explore** shows one folder level as a treemap. Striped tiles are disposable, translucent tiles are restorable, solid tiles are your data, and a lock marks space macOS manages. Double-click a folder to open it.
- **Recommended** lists what DiskLens found, split into *Safe to remove* and *Review first*.

## Your first cleanup

1. In Recommended, tick an item, or press Space on a tile in Explore. Items you stage collect in the queue.
2. Click **Clean up**. DiskLens lists exactly what will happen to each item.
3. Click the confirm button. Pressing Return does not confirm.

Nothing is staged when DiskLens opens, and nothing runs until you confirm. Read [How cleanup works](/docs/how-cleanup-works) for the details.

## Rescans

DiskLens shows your last results at launch and updates them in the background. If the data is more than a day old it rescans on its own. Press <kbd>⌘R</kbd> to rescan now.
